# Step 1: Research Document (`research.md`)

**Date:** November 22, 2024  
**Project:** Kirana Stockout & Expiry Early-Warning System  
**Person:** Rajinder Kumar (Shopkeeper)  
**Shop Name:** Rajinder Karyana Store  
**Location:** Local Village Grocery Store (Rural/Semi-urban)  

---

## 1. The Problem (in 5 lines)
1. Small village kirana stores suffer a severe dual inventory trap: sudden stockouts of daily essentials and quiet financial losses from expiring packaged foods.
2. The shopkeeper, Rajinder Kumar, relies purely on visual memory and distributor sales pitches rather than structured inventory tracking.
3. Fast-moving staples (like milk, salt, butter, and oil) run out unexpectedly mid-week, causing immediate revenue loss and sending village customers to rival stores.
4. Concurrently, wholesale distributors push bulk carton deals (bread, biscuits, namkeen) with distributor discounts, resulting in slow-moving items getting pushed to back shelves and expiring unnoticed.
5. Because Rajinder is not formally tech-educated and distrusts complex internet cloud services, he needs a transparent, simple, local visual tool that flags what to order and what to sell immediately.

---

## 2. Two Existing Solutions & Why They Do Not Fit Rajinder Kumar

### Solution A: Khatabook / Vyapar (Mobile Accounting Apps)
* **What they do:** Popular smartphone applications focused on digital ledger (bahi-khata), debt recovery SMS, and GST invoicing.
* **Why they do not fit Rajinder:** 
  - They are heavily centered on bookkeeping and credit collection rather than proactive shelf-life monitoring and predictive reorder warnings.
  - They demand extensive smartphone typing, multi-step navigation, and constant internet connectivity.
  - Rajinder finds mobile accounting interfaces intimidating and cluttered; he voiced deep skepticism towards internet-based platforms holding his business data.

### Solution B: Zoho Inventory / Enterprise Retail POS Systems
* **What they do:** Comprehensive, cloud-based inventory software with automated purchase orders and barcode scanning.
* **Why they do not fit Rajinder:**
  - They require dedicated barcode scanners, thermal receipt printers, and monthly SaaS subscriptions that are unviable for a single-counter village shop.
  - A substantial portion of Rajinder’s inventory includes locally packed loose staples (e.g. 1kg polybags of loose Toor Dal and Sugar packed in-house) which have no standard EAN/UPC barcodes.
  - The software is overly complex, laden with accounting jargon, and assumes high digital literacy.

## 3. Two Sources Read (with 3-line notes each)

### Source 1: "The Kirana Store Transformation: Challenges and Opportunities in Rural Retail" (Indian Journal of Marketing & Supply Chain)
* **Note 1:** Documents that over 65% of rural Indian mom-and-pop retailers operate without inventory buffers, depending entirely on distributor visit cycles.
* **Note 2:** Explains that distributor trade schemes often push excess stock of short-shelf-life goods onto rural shops, leading to 4–7% inventory write-offs.
* **Note 3:** Demonstrates that running out of top staples (milk, salt, cooking oil) causes direct customer diversion to neighboring village shops.

### Source 2: "FMCG Distribution in Rural India: Shelf-Life Management and Wastage Patterns" (FICCI Retail Industry Report)
* **Note 1:** Identifies packaged bakery, dairy pouches, and confectionery as the top three categories prone to unmonitored retail expiration.
* **Note 2:** Notes that small shop owners resist digital tools because of complex workflows, English-only interfaces, and fear of cloud data leakage.
* **Note 3:** Recommends minimal visual interfaces—specifically high-contrast traffic-light cues (Red/Yellow/Green)—as the most effective design for non-tech-educated operators.
