## 2. In-Depth Conversation with Rajinder Kumar

### Context
Rajinder uncle has been running this village store since 2010. He does not have a computer; he uses a basic Android smartphone primarily for WhatsApp and YouTube. All store transactions are cash or occasional local UPI. He uses a paper notebook solely for customer udhaar (credit) and writes distributor order reminders on discarded cardboard or cigarette cartons.

### 3 Key Things Rajinder Said

1. **On Stock Wastage & Distributor Traps:**
   > *"Some items get stuck on the shelves because distributors lure me with schemes—like take 3 dozen Modern bread or sweet biscuits for ₹50 extra discount. But in our village, families buy fresh roti, so sliced bread sells very slowly. By the time 10 packets sell, the remaining 9 packets cross their expiry date. That single expired carton wipes out my entire week's profit on bakery items."*

2. **On Sudden Stockouts & Lost Customers:**
   > *"Fast items like Tata Salt and Amul Butter run out without me noticing. Just yesterday, two women came asking for 1kg Tata Salt. When I reached into the shelf, the box was empty! In a village, if someone doesn't get salt at my shop, they walk 100 meters to Sharmaji's shop, and I lose that customer for their entire grocery basket."*

3. **On Technology & Distrust of Internet Apps:**
   > *"Sales agents came twice telling me to install Vyapar or Khatabook. I tried once, but it asked for GST numbers, bank sync, and daily bill entry in English. I do not have time to type on a glass screen when 5 customers are shouting their orders. Plus, I don't trust internet companies holding my daily numbers. But you are from our village—if you make something clean and simple that shows me what will finish and what will spoil, I will gladly use it."*

## 3. The One Big Thing That Surprised Me

> **What I assumed before going:**  
> I thought stockouts happened because customers bought unexpectedly large quantities in sudden bursts.
>
> **The surprising reality:**  
> Stockouts happen entirely because of **visual blind spots and fixed distributor schedules**. 
> - Wholesale distributors only visit once a week (Tuesdays for dairy/bakery, Fridays for staples).
> - When the distributor arrives, Rajinder stands behind the counter and does a quick 30-second eye scan of whatever is directly in front of him. 
> - If an item is stocked in the **deep freezer** (Amul butter) or stacked in **bottom floor crates** (Tata salt), he cannot see it from the counter. He mistakenly assumes he still has inventory left. By Thursday night, he sells the last pack, and he cannot get any new stock until the following week's delivery!

## 4. How the Raw Data (`data.csv`) Was Collected

- **Methodology:** Physical shelf-by-shelf audit conducted directly behind the counter alongside Rajinder.
- **Process:**
  1. We physically counted units across 5 specific shop zones: Front Glass Counter, Hanging Strip Racks, Middle Wooden Shelves, Floor Storage Crates, and Deep Freezer.
  2. We cross-verified purchase prices from his recent physical paper wholesale slips spiked on his counter nail.
  3. We checked the printed manufacturing and expiry dates on each batch (e.g., Modern Bread expiring Nov 26, Haldiram Bhujia expiring Dec 4, Mother Dairy Dahi expiring Dec 2).
  4. Non-round numbers reflect actual physical counts (e.g., 7 packs of Parle-G, 3 milk pouches, 13 oil pouches).
  5. Included authentic ground realities: e.g., Item `ITM014` (Everest Turmeric) had 1 pack damaged due to a rat-nibbled corner on the bottom shelf; Item `ITM005` (Tata Salt) and `ITM015` (Amul Butter) were completely out of stock (`0` units).
